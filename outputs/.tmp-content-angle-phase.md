# Content Strategy — Phase 3 (content-angle-strategist)

Input: offer-architect Phase 2 handoff (`/home/user/selenium/outputs/.tmp-offer-architect-phase.md`). Reasoned independently from the older `final-video-brief.md` draft, which predates the corrected audience/urgency scoping and is not carried forward except where it happens to still hold up.

Governing constraints carried into every decision below:
- Clickthrough-driven concept (Searchability 2/5) — hook/thumbnail/retention get the investment, not SEO phrasing.
- Buyer is solo consultants/coaches/service-based creators who already use AI in their business — not engineers chasing the subagent pattern. Technical vocabulary ("Claude Code," "AI agents," "subagents") is kept out of title and thumbnail, and subordinated in the video body.
- No manufactured urgency. The licensed timing argument is trust/category-timing (AI-slop skepticism is real and current; this pairing isn't oversold yet) — not "this week," not acute personal pain.
- The Dependency Gate — visible, repeatable rejection — is the entire credibility budget. It gets real, unhurried, unedited screen time, not a narrated aside.
- No "$15K strategist," no unscoped dollar comparisons.
- The promise is coherence (five assets built to fit each other), not clients or revenue.

## Content Strategy

### Best Title

**"If You Want Your AI Drafts To Actually Match, Build This First"**

Why it wins: it uses the exact requested angle pattern ("If you want [desired outcome], build this first"), and the desired outcome it names — drafts that actually match each other — is the one thing this offer is honestly licensed to promise (coherence, not clients or revenue). It is legible in under 3 seconds, contains zero tool/agent vocabulary, and it directly targets the stated audience's live symptom (disconnected positioning/offer/content drafts sitting in a doc) without needing to explain a mechanism to make sense. It also avoids the buyer/clicker trap: an engineer scanning titles has no reason to click this one, while someone with three mismatched AI drafts open in tabs right now does.

### Backup Titles

1. "Why Your AI Positioning, Offer, and Content Never Match"
2. "I Made AI Reject My Own Business Idea"
3. "Stop Running Your Offer Through Five Separate AI Chats"
4. "You're Not Behind On AI — You're Missing One Step"
5. "The One Thing Missing From Every AI Business Prompt"

### Thumbnail Text

1. "AI Said No."
2. "It Rejected Me."
3. "Watch It Say No"
4. "AI Drafts Don't Match"
5. "One Missing Step"

Note: none of these name the mechanism ("agents," "gate," "Claude Code") — they carry the curiosity/trust angle (something got rejected, something's missing) that pulls the target buyer without pulling in clicker-only traffic chasing a tool pattern.

### Hook (0:00–0:20, word for word)

"If you've ever run your positioning through ChatGPT, your offer through another chat, and your content ideas through a third — you already know the problem. None of it matches. It reads like three different people wrote it, because three different sessions did. I'm not going to give you another AI prompt. I'm going to show you the one rule that makes AI refuse to hand you something vague — live, on a real business, right now."

This is about the viewer's outcome (drafts that fit together) and their already-felt frustration (disconnected AI sessions), not about the tool or the word "agent." The word "refuse" plants the proof beat without naming the mechanism yet.

## Cut For Filler, Theory, Or Generic-Tutorial Risk

- No standalone "here's what AI agents are" explainer — the mechanism is shown mechanically (arrows, pulled phrases) rather than lectured.
- No setup/installation/prompting-mechanics walkthrough — compressed to the 20-second setup beat; the audience needs to see judgment (the reject/refine), not configuration.
- No third objection ("AI output is generic") — already answered live by the reject/refine beat; cutting it protects the CTA's runtime instead.
- No dollar-value comparison anywhere in hook, title, thumbnail, or CTA.
- No "this week" / scarcity language in the CTA — the timing argument used is category-level (skepticism is current, this pairing is thin), not personal urgency.
