## Demand-Signal Assets (Lead-Magnet Equivalent, Adapted)

A traditional lead magnet is a discrete downloadable asset traded for an email address. Nothing in the brief supports that mechanic — no email capture tool, no opt-in form, no PDF/guide is named, and the paid guide is explicitly gated behind proof that doesn't exist yet **(Evidence — brief, line 21; Phase 2, Value Ladder rung 4)**. Building a PDF here would both invent an unauthorized mechanism and risk sounding like generic funnel-template output, which line 41 explicitly forbids. So the two "assets" below are not new deliverables — they are existing content restructured to *function* as the lead-magnet-equivalent, plus one clearly flagged optional extension.

### 1. Affiliate line: "The Rotation" (the Sunday slot itself, as a standing asset)

- **Name:** Not a new name for a new thing — "what's in rotation" is the brief's own sanctioned phrase **(Evidence — brief, line 34)**, reused as the *asset identity* rather than a one-off caption line. Every Sunday becomes an installment of the same recognizable, recurring artifact.
- **Promise:** Not "get my discount code" — the promise is continuity: "the pieces that survived more than one wear, reviewed honestly, once a week." This satisfies the no-haul/no-discount constraint **(Evidence — brief, line 34)** and the 90-day patience rule **(Evidence — brief, line 39)** by framing the asset as cumulative proof over time, not a single conversion event.
- **Contents:** Exactly what Phase 3 already specified for Sunday — reflection beat, one folded-in rotation mention, one link reference, save/share invitation, no metrics. **(Evidence — Phase 3, Sunday section, lines 79–93)**. I am not adding new content to this slot; I am naming the existing structure as the asset so the creator (and any future collaborator) can recognize it as the thing that does lead-magnet work, without literally building a lead magnet.
- **Why someone wants it now (Inference):** because it is the only place all week where the "why" behind three days of outfit content gets synthesized and the link becomes low-friction to act on — someone who saved Tuesday's Reel and Wednesday's carousel has, by Sunday, three data points converging into one answer. This is a reasonable extension of the brief's own sequencing (Tue → Wed → Sat → Sun) **(Evidence — brief, line 21)**, not a claim about actual audience behavior, since no engagement data exists in the brief to confirm it **(Evidence — Phase 2, Proof Requirements, explicit gap)**.

### 2. Braise Beauty line: "The Save Is the Signal" (reusing the brief's own success metric as demand capture)

**(Evidence — brief, line 39)**: "Do not judge results before 90 days; saves and shares matter more than likes." This is the most direct, already-authorized proxy for demand the brief provides. Rather than inventing a waitlist, the most disciplined move is to treat saves, shares, and DM replies on Braise Beauty BTS content as the demand signal itself — no new tool, no new mechanism, fully consistent with a rule already in the brief.

- **What this looks like in practice (Inference, low-risk extension):** BTS posts continue exactly as Phase 3 specified — no CTA to buy, preorder, or join a list **(Evidence — Phase 3, Braise Beauty section, "Explicit exclusion")** — and the informal signal (which BTS posts get saved/shared/DM'd about) is simply *observed*, not solicited with new copy. This requires zero new content, zero new CTA, and cannot violate the pillar's no-CTA rule because nothing is being added to the posts themselves.

- **Optional extension — flagged explicitly (Inference — proposed, not yet validated or authorized by the brief):** if and when more structured signal becomes useful, an Instagram Stories "highlight" archive of BTS moments (e.g., titled around the brief's own language, "Building It") could let repeat visitors self-select into watching the founding-stage story in sequence. A story sticker (poll/question type) used occasionally — *not weekly, not as a caption CTA* — could ask something like "would this work on your skin tone?" which doubles as product-development signal for the secondary audience **(Evidence — brief, line 7)** and as an engagement action. **I am flagging this clearly: the brief does not name a Stories highlight system, a sticker cadence, or any Braise Beauty interest-capture mechanism. ## Friction Check

Self-critical, as instructed — listing everything in this system that is inference rather than brief-authorized fact, and where it could feel confusing, forced, or premature if implemented as-is:

1. **The "optional extension" (Stories highlight + occasional sticker) is the single riskiest item in this document.** It is clearly labeled Inference, but any collaborator skimming quickly could mistake it for an existing feature. I recommend the coordinator keep it visually separated and marked "not authorized" in the final deliverable, not blended into the main asset list.

2. **Tension with Phase 3's explicit "no CTA anywhere in the BTS pillar" rule.** My "Save Is the Signal" mechanism avoids adding a CTA to BTS posts, but the optional sticker idea sits close to that line even though I placed it in Stories rather than in-feed. If the creator or coordinator is not careful, this could drift into a de facto BTS CTA, which the brief-derived Phase 3 output explicitly rules out. Safer default: do not implement the optional extension at all until Braise Beauty has an actual product to point to.

3. **"Five Sundays" as a follow-up sequence is itself an adaptation, not a brief-stated structure.** The brief describes Sunday as a recurring weekly slot, not a numbered five-part campaign. Numbering it W1–W5 risks implying a campaign structure/escalation that doesn't exist in her actual content rhythm, which just repeats indefinitely. The coordinator should present this as "five representative weeks of the same ongoing slot," not a finite campaign with an end state.

4. **No performance data exists anywhere in the brief or Phase 1/2/3 outputs to confirm any of this will produce saves, shares, or clicks.** Every "why this works" reasoning above is Inference from voice/positioning fit, not validated behavior. Presenting this system as reliably effective would misrepresent the brief's own acknowledged gap. **(Evidence — Phase 2, Proof Requirements, explicit gap; inherited from Phase 1)**

5. **Naming things ("The Rotation," "The Save Is the Signal") risks sounding like funnel-marketing branding layered onto a personal, documentary voice.** I chose these as internal/strategic labels for this document, not as copy she should ever say on camera or in captions — the only actual on-screen/caption language in this system is the CTA and the five hooks, which were written to pass the brief's voice rules. The coordinator should make clear these framework names are scaffolding, not content.

6. **The affiliate side of this system requires no new authorization** — it is a direct restatement of Phase 2/3's already-approved mechanics. The Braise Beauty side is where all the invented-mechanism risk concentrates, and I've tried to minimize that by defaulting to "reuse the existing save/share metric" rather than proposing new infrastructure — but the coordinator should still flag to the creator that no Braise Beauty conversion mechanism is live today, full stop.

7. **90-day rule interaction:** Week 5's hook ("One month of what's actually working") walks close to a "results" statement. I judged it acceptable because it describes *consistency of behavior* (what stayed in rotation) rather than *outcomes* (sales, growth, revenue) — but this is a judgment call, not a bright line the brief draws itself, and should be treated as such if reviewed.
