# Offer Architecture — Phase 2 (offer-architect)

Input: market-signal-researcher brief (Opportunity Score 25/35: Urgency 3/5, WTP 4/5, Searchability 2/5, Clickability 4/5, Credibility 5/5, Business fit 4/5, Under-15-min viability 3/5).

This pass makes three explicit calls the research flagged as open, before the offer itself:

---

## Explicit Call 1 — Who The Audience Actually Is (Buyer/Clicker Split)

The research is right that "coaches with inbound DMs" was a silent default in prior work, and that it collides with the buyer/clicker risk: a technical hook (Claude Code, subagents) pulls engineers/AI hobbyists who watch the mechanism for free and never buy, while a pure-outcome hook competes in the saturated "build a business with AI" cluster with no differentiation.

**Call: the audience is solo consultants, coaches, and service-based creators who already use AI tools in their own business — not AI-naive, not professional engineers.** Specifically: people who have already run their positioning through ChatGPT, their offer through another session, and a lead magnet through a third, and have the disconnected drafts sitting in a doc right now. This is the researcher's own "bridging" population, stated as a decision rather than left open.

Why this resolves the split instead of just naming it:
- It explains why they'd click a video with a visible agent/chain mechanism (they're AI-curious builders, not tool-naive) — so the hook doesn't have to hide the mechanism, it has to subordinate it to outcome in framing (per the research's own recommendation).
- It explains why they're a plausible buyer (they have a live business decision — positioning/offer/funnel — not just curiosity about subagents).
- It explicitly excludes two adjacent groups the content will still attract but should not be written for: (a) engineers who want the subagent pattern for its own sake — they are welcome traffic, not the target, and the CTA should not chase them; (b) fully AI-naive coaches with zero tool experience — the free lead magnet still works for them, but the *hook* and *voice* assume some prior AI-tool frustration, not AI-tool absence.

This is a targeting decision the content-angle-strategist should carry forward: write the hook for someone who has already tried AI for their business and been unimpressed, not someone discovering AI for the first time, and not someone chasing the subagent pattern itself.

---

## Explicit Call 2 — Scoping The Promise To What's Evidenced

The research scored Urgency at 3/5 ("chronic not acute, evidence-light") and flagged that a prior pass's "your DMs are leaking money this week" framing was inference, not evidence. This pass does not inherit that confidence.

**What is evidenced and can be claimed:**
- AI-slop skepticism is real and current (Hank Green backlash, "provenance is the new content currency"). This licenses a *trust* angle — "here's why your last AI attempt produced generic output" — with real backing.
- The "Claude Code for coaches/consultants" pairing is emerging in 2026 but still thin. This licenses a *category-timing* angle — the audience hasn't been oversold this specific mechanism yet — not a *personal-urgency* angle.
- WTP (4/5) and Clickability (4/5) are stronger, but WTP was scored on inference, not public evidence — so pricing below is framed as a hypothesis to validate, not a settled figure.

**What is not evidenced and will not be claimed:** any version of "attention is leaking right now," "every day without this costs you a client," or a specific dollar-value comparison (e.g., "$15K strategist"). Nothing in the research supports acute, individual urgency — only chronic mismatch (disconnected AI drafts) plus category-level timing (trust gap + thin content pairing). The offer below is built on the chronic-pain-plus-live-proof combination, not manufactured urgency.

**Why now (evidence-scoped, not inferred):** not "your revenue is leaking this week" — instead, "the market has just turned skeptical of exactly the failure mode your last AI attempt produced (disconnected, generic, ungated output), and the mechanism that fixes it is not yet a crowded pitch." That is a real, current, defensible timing argument; a personal-urgency one is not.

## Positioning

I help solo consultants, coaches, and service-based creators who already use AI tools in their own business achieve one coherent set of go-to-market assets — a positioning statement, an offer, a content angle, a lead magnet, and a follow-up sequence that are provably built to fit each other — without hiring a strategist or re-running the same disconnected ChatGPT sessions that keep producing pieces that don't match, by building a dependency-gated five-agent chain where no agent is allowed to hand its output forward until it's specific enough for the next agent to build on.

Note what this positioning does *not* promise: it does not promise more clients, more revenue, or a solved funnel problem — those are downstream possibilities, not what's demonstrated. What's demonstrated is coherence: five artifacts that were built to fit each other instead of stitched together after the fact. That is the honest, provable claim given a 3/5 urgency score and no public evidence of a revenue outcome.

---

## Core Offer

**The Dependency Gate Chain** — a five-agent build (Positioning → Offer Architect → Content Angle → Lead Magnet → Follow-Up Sequence) where each agent hard-rejects input from the agent before it if that input isn't specific enough to build on. The product being sold is not "AI-generated content" — it's *coherence*, proven by watching the system refuse to accept a vague answer live.

- **Free tier:** the self-run chain, gates included, run on your own business.
- **Paid tier (workshop, hypothesis-priced $197–$397 — validate via presale before committing production spend, since WTP here is inferred, not evidenced):** the same chain, run live on the buyer's own business, with the gate criteria exposed so they can see *why* an input was rejected and fix it themselves.
- **Premium tier (done-with-you, hypothesis-priced $3K–$8K):** the chain extended past these five agents into the buyer's actual funnel (pricing, sales page, ad/DM sequence), with a strategist in the loop. This tier should not be built out or marketed hard until the workshop tier has actual paying conversions — the research gives no evidence this audience will pay at this level, only that the shape is plausible.

This offer deliberately does not claim to replace a $15K strategist or promise a revenue outcome. It claims to end a specific, visible failure: disconnected AI drafts that don't fit together. That is what's provable on camera.

---

## Unique Mechanism

**The Dependency Gate.** Each agent in the chain is constrained to only accept input that has already passed a specificity check from the agent before it — vague input is rejected and sent back, not silently accepted and softened. The mechanism is not "multiple AI agents" (that's now a commodity framing, actively covered in current subagent content) — it's that *rejection is visible and repeatable*, not a one-off. The proof isn't the generation step; it's the refusal step.

This is also the honest differentiator against category fatigue: "free template → paid workshop → DWY sprint" is a common ladder shape in adjacent content now (researcher-flagged). The ladder itself carries no differentiation. The gate — specifically, that it can be shown rejecting a real, unscripted input on camera — is the only thing that does.

---

## Value Ladder

Free video (watch the chain reject and refine a real input, live, unedited across that beat) → free lead magnet (the self-run gated template) → paid workshop (same chain, gates explained, run live on the buyer's business) → premium done-with-you sprint (chain extended into the buyer's actual funnel, gated on workshop demand actually materializing).

Explicitly note for the next phase (conversion-system-builder): because the ladder shape itself is not differentiated (category fatigue is a named risk), the copy at every rung must reuse the exact words "gate" and "rejected" from the video — the ladder's credibility comes from continuity with the proof moment, not from the ladder's novelty.

---

## Proof Requirements

What must be on screen, in order of importance:

1. **The reject/refine beat must read as unstaged. This is not optional and not a stylistic preference — it is the direct answer to a named, evidenced risk (AI-slop/provenance skepticism, real and current per the Hank Green backlash and "provenance is the new content currency").** Concretely, this means:
   - A genuine, unscripted input — not a pre-written "bad example" planted to be rejected. If the input was chosen in advance to fail, the video should not pretend otherwise; better to use the creator's *own* real, first-attempt input and let it fail live if it does, or be transparent on screen if a viewer-submitted example is used.
   - The rejection reason must be shown as actual gate criteria (a visible rule or check), not just narrated ("yeah that's too vague, let me redo it") — narration alone is exactly what will read as staged theater.
   - No jump cut across the reject moment itself. If the take needs editing elsewhere, this specific beat should run in as close to real time as the format allows.
   - Ideally more than one rejection is shown across the five agents (even briefly) — a single, suspiciously clean reject-then-perfect-pass reads as choreography; a second, smaller rejection later in the chain reads as a system behaving consistently, not a one-time bit.
2. The dependency chain shown mechanically, not just claimed: one agent's actual output pasted or referenced as the next agent's input, visible on screen, every time.
3. The full five-artifact before/after (one-line starting idea vs. all five finished pieces) shown once, with zero placeholder or bracket text (no "[niche]" left unfilled) — bracket text left on screen is a smaller-scale version of the same provenance problem.
4. A specific business example on screen (a named or clearly-typed niche, not a generic "your business") — the audience defined above has already seen enough generic AI demos to distrust anything that stays abstract.

## Objection Map

| Objection | Who raises it | How the video answers it |
|---|---|---|
| "I already tried AI for my positioning/offer and got generic garbage." | Core audience (AI-curious builders) | Named directly and reframed: the failure mode was an ungated single-shot prompt, not "AI doesn't work" — shown, not just asserted, via the live reject/refine beat. |
| "This looks staged — nobody's AI output is *this* neatly rejected then fixed." | Skeptical viewers generally (provenance-skepticism climate) | Addressed structurally, not verbally — see Proof Requirements above. This objection is pre-empted by *how* the beat is filmed, not by a line of dialogue defending it. |
| "Isn't this just ChatGPT/Claude with extra steps? I already know subagents exist." | Technical/clicker segment (not the buyer) | Not over-addressed — this is the researcher's flagged buyer/clicker split. The video should not spend CTA time convincing engineers to buy; instead, the mechanism explanation is framed around the *business decision* each gate checks for (is this positioning specific enough to act on?), which is the part a generic subagent tutorial doesn't cover. This lets the technical framing stay subordinate without being hidden. |
| "Does this actually get me clients / revenue?" | Core audience, most important objection to not overclaim on | Answered honestly, not oversold: no, it does not guarantee clients — it guarantees the five assets that catch attention are built to fit each other, which is what's provable on screen. Overclaiming revenue here is the single biggest way this offer becomes not-credible, given urgency/WTP were inference-scored, not evidence-scored. |
| "Why pay for the workshop if the free template runs the whole chain anyway?" | Warm leads considering the paid tier | Named plainly, not hidden: the free chain rejects vague input but doesn't explain the criteria — the workshop opens the gates so the buyer learns to pass them without the tool, not just this once. |
| "This is too broad — will it work for my specific type of business?" | Any viewer evaluating fit | Requires at minimum one specific, named example on screen (Proof Requirement 4) rather than staying abstract; the free template's gate criteria should be generic enough to apply broadly but the on-screen demo must not be. |

## Handoff Notes For content-angle-strategist

- Treat this as clickthrough-driven, not search-driven, per the research (Searchability 2/5) — invest retention/hook design accordingly, not SEO.
- Do not write the hook around "AI agents" or "Claude Code subagents" as the headline draw; subordinate the technical mechanism to the outcome (coherent, fit-together assets), consistent with the audience call above.
- Do not manufacture urgency language ("this week," "leaking money now") — the honest urgency angle is the trust/timing one (category is thin, skepticism is high, this format answers it live), not personal acute pain.
- The single most important retention/proof beat to plan for is the unstaged reject/refine moment — budget it real screen time and do not let editing shortcuts undercut it.
