# Braise Beauty Revenue Plan

Coordinator output combining: market-signal-researcher → offer-architect → content-angle-strategist → conversion-system-builder, run against `business-brief.md` (Braise Beauty).

## Executive Summary

Braise Beauty sits at the intersection of two proven demand patterns: real, growing search demand for undertone-correct beauty products for deeper skin tones (a market currently served mostly by legacy brands' shade *extensions*, not founder-native formulation), and the "build in public" documentary trust @ma.milann has already built. Opportunity score: **28/35**. The audience has been burned before by "inclusive" ranges that are just a darker mid-tone base — so the entire plan is built around one differentiator competitors can't fake: **showing the undertone-testing process fail on camera, not just succeed.** The lead magnet, content, and follow-up all exist to prove that one claim.

## Content Angle

**Reel hook:** *"I rejected this shade three times before I understood why."*

This opens on conflict, not a pitch — it directly answers the audience's core skepticism (another recolored mid-tone base) by showing a real rejection before any product is sold.

**Backup hooks:** "This is the shade every 'inclusive' brand would've shipped. I didn't." · "Undertone vs. depth — the difference nobody explains to you" · "Why I said no to this batch on camera" · "You're not imagining it — most 'deeper shade' ranges are the same base, darker"

**Cover text options:** "Rejected." / "Warm ≠ Deep." / "Not sold. Shown." / "Batch 3." / "Still wrong."

## Why This Should Work

- **Credibility: 5/5** — her own hands, her own undertone, her own visible process. Close to unfakeable.
- **Business fit: 5/5** — quiz → early access → launch is a coherent funnel with no offer invented for content's sake.
- **The wedge:** undertone (warm/rust/olive) vs. depth is the most sophisticated, least-used distinction in this market — and it's exactly what "Warm. Ember. You." is built to say.
- **The risk to guard against:** this goes generic the moment the process gets sanitized. A rejected batch shown honestly is the single highest-credibility, lowest-cost move available — a polished "finally, a brand for us" moment is worth nothing here.
- **Realistic pace:** measure saves, shares, and quiz completions against the existing audience — not cold-search follower growth. The 90-day patience rule applies.

## The Offer

**Positioning:** Braise Beauty is the first deeper-skin-tone beauty line formulated from an undertone up — not a darker recolor of a lighter-skin default — and the only one letting you watch every shade-match decision get made, rejected, and remade in public before you buy.

**Core Offer:** Founding line early access (lip oil, liner, blush compact) via the shade-match quiz. No discount codes, ever — early access to limited founding stock is the entire incentive.

**Value Ladder:** Free documentary Reels → shade-match quiz (lead magnet) → founding line launch to early-access list → expanded shade range built directly from waitlist quiz data.

## The Unique Mechanism

**The Undertone-First Formulation Process** — source → swatch against real undertone references (warm/rust/olive/deep-neutral) on actual skin → reject/approve → document why, on camera. It's credible specifically *because* rejected batches and unresolved mismatches are shown, not edited out. This is the direct answer to "just a darkened mid-tone base."

## Lead Magnet

**The Batch Map — Undertone Match Quiz.** 6–8 questions using the same undertone references shown in the Reels. The result gives three things: undertone category, that category's live batch status (approved / in testing / rejected-and-restarting), and a mini "rejection archive" tied to their specific undertone with the on-camera reasoning for each rejection. Wanted immediately because the Reel ends unresolved — the quiz is the only place "does my undertone belong to something solved or unsolved?" gets answered.

## In-Reel CTA

> "Comment MATCH and I'll DM you the same undertone reference I just used on this batch — so you can see exactly where yours falls. Matched, still testing, or not solved yet. Quiz is linked in bio too if DMs are off for you."

Cover text (last 2 seconds): **"Comment MATCH — see where your undertone falls"**

## Follow-Up Sequence

1. **Immediate DM** — deliver the quiz result with zero pitch: undertone category + current batch status. CTA: reply ARCHIVE to see rejected batches for that undertone.
2. **Day 1–2 DM** — show 2–3 real rejected-batch clips specific to their undertone, with the actual on-camera reasoning. CTA: hold a founding spot by undertone category.
3. **Day 4–5 DM** — a genuine progress update (approved, or still stuck) — including a real delay if that's what happened. CTA: confirm queue spot.
4. **Day 7–9 DM** — ask for email, framed as protecting a limited, undertone-segmented queue position, not a newsletter signup.
5. **Day 12–14 email** — "Your undertone's batch just got approved." Recaps the specific rejections they already saw, confirms approval, states real unit count. CTA: claim founding access before general release — no code, just early access.

**Sales path:** the quiz segments by undertone so every follow-up speaks to that person's *specific* batch — not a generic list. When a category's batch is approved, that segment gets notified first. The aggregate quiz data (which undertones show the most "still testing" volume) becomes the real R&D input for the expanded range, launched back to the same list.

## What Must Be Shown To Be Credible

- Her actual hands and face doing sourcing and swatch-testing — never a stock or studio shot.
- At least one on-screen rejected batch or undertone mismatch left genuinely unresolved, not neatly fixed in the same post.
- Real undertone comparisons on actual skin — never a color-wheel graphic.
- A visible link between quiz answers/DMs and real product decisions ("three of you said this reads olive — here's what changed").
- Timeline honesty: delays and reformulations shown as they happen, not narrated retroactively as a clean success story.

## What To Cut Because It's Generic

- Any "finally, a brand for us" inclusive-beauty ad language — the audience has heard it from every legacy brand's shade extension.
- Polished founder-lifestyle content that edits out real product friction — the imperfect process is the entire credibility asset.
- A color-wheel graphic in place of a real skin comparison.
- Manufactured urgency ("only 3 spots left!") layered on top of real stock scarcity — if a number isn't real and specific, don't state one.
- Any discount-code or "shop now" language anywhere in the Reel, DM sequence, or email — even accidentally in an automated template.
- Bot-cheerful DM tone — automated messages must stay as flat and specific as the Reel narration, or the documentary credibility breaks on contact.
