## Positioning

I help solo coaches and consultants who already have paying clients — from referrals, DMs, word of mouth — but no repeatable way to convert the next one, get a validated positioning statement, offer, lead magnet, and 5-email follow-up sequence, built and stress-tested for specificity by an AI agent team in one sitting. No $15K–25K brand strategist. No three months rewriting the same vague ChatGPT prompt into something that still sounds like everyone else's.

## Why This Topic Works Now

- **Willingness to pay: 5/5** — tied directly to positioning, offers, and leads, not abstract productivity.
- **Credibility: 5/5** — provable live, on camera, in real time. The format is proof, not a claim.
- **Business fit: 5/5** — the video's artifact *is* the product being sold.
- **The audience:** not AI-curious beginners. They've already tried AI on their own offer and gotten something too vague to ship — they don't need convincing AI is useful, they need proof this output won't be generic.
- **Why now:** as more competitors publish AI-written content, everyone's positioning is starting to sound the same. A vague offer no longer means slow growth — it means disappearing into a feed full of interchangeable AI voices. Sharpness just became a survival trait.

## Core Offer

**The Revenue Agent System** — a paid agent-team template + workshop. Five agents (Positioning, Offer Architect, Content Angle, Lead Magnet, Follow-Up Sequence), each constrained to consume *only* the validated output of the agent before it. The deliverable isn't "AI content" — it's a positioning-to-offer-to-lead-magnet chain that already survived a rejection pass before the viewer ever sees it.

**Value Ladder:** Free video → free lead magnet (the template) → $197–497 workshop → $3K–10K done-with-you AI Systems Sprint.

## The Unique Mechanism

**The Revenue Dependency Chain.** No agent is allowed to freelance — each one only works with what the previous agent already proved was specific enough to pass. The proof moment on camera isn't the generation, it's watching the system catch and reject a generic, vague output and force a specific one. That single beat is what separates this from "ChatGPT with extra steps."

## Lead Magnet

**The Revenue Agent System Template (Free Build)** — the exact 5-agent chain from the video, pre-built and pre-wired. Includes the 5 agent files with the reject/refine logic already baked in, a no-experience-needed setup doc, and a short "why this isn't just ChatGPT" note. Wanted immediately because the viewer just watched the reject/refine moment happen on someone else's business — the template is the only way to get that same moment on their own.

## In-Video CTA

> "If you want to run this exact system on your own business — same five agents, same reject-and-refine chain, nothing to build from scratch — I put the whole template below. Grab it, plug in your business, and watch it reject the first generic thing it tries to hand you. That's how you'll know it's working."

## 5-Email Follow-Up Sequence

1. **Delivery** — *"Here's your Revenue Agent System (start here)."* Deliver the template, set the expectation that the first rejection is the system working, not a bug. CTA: run Agent 1 today, reply with what it rejected.
2. **Reframe the objection** — *"Why your last AI tool gave you generic junk."* Names "I already tried AI tools" and reframes it as a sequencing problem. CTA: template link again.
3. **Show the chain working** — *"What happens when Agent 2 gets a weak handoff."* A concrete before/after showing a clean dependency handoff. CTA: reply with where you are in the chain.
4. **Name the ceiling honestly** — *"The template gets you 80% of the way there."* States plainly what the free system won't do (troubleshooting, extending the chain). No hard pitch. CTA: workshop link for anyone who's run the full chain.
5. **Direct invite** — *"Ready to run this on repeat?"* Low-pressure invite for people who've already felt the "aha." CTA: see workshop details.

**Sales path:** the lead magnet is the actual conversion event — once someone's own input gets rejected by Agent 1, they've personally felt the offer's core claim, which sells harder than any pitch. The sequence keeps them moving through the chain, then names the free system's honest ceiling before ever mentioning price.

## What Must Be Shown On Screen To Be Credible

- Agent 1 built live and in full — not summarized, not "here's one I made earlier."
- A real, visible reject-then-refine moment on a generic output.
- The dependency chain shown visually — one agent's exact output referenced or pasted as the next agent's input, not just claimed in narration.
- The before/after: the one-line starting idea next to all five finished artifacts, zero placeholder text anywhere.
- Real, specific content in the offer formula on screen — never a bracket like "[niche]" left unfilled.

## What Was Cut Because It Felt Generic

- The standalone "preview the 5 outputs" beat — it duplicated the before/after payoff and cost time the proof section needed more.
- Any framing of the agent team as a novelty instead of anchored to a revenue decision.
- Lengthy setup/installation/prompting mechanics — compressed to 20 seconds; the audience needs judgment shown, not configuration.
- A separate, back-to-back objection-handling segment — folded into the offer beat so objections land as the reason the free system exists, not as defense.
- "$15K strategist" used as an unscoped, aspirational claim — rescoped to describe exactly what the video proves, not what the full offer implies.
